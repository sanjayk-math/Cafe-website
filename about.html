Our story

Started with one borrowed oven

Hearth & Rye began as a Saturday market stall in 2019, when Nora Fielding started bringing loaves baked in a wood-fired oven she shared with three other bakers before dawn. What started as forty loaves a week grew into something that needed its own hearth — so in 2022, Hearth & Rye opened its doors on Miller Street.

How we got here

  • 2019

    A market stall and a shared oven

    Nora starts baking sourdough for the Saturday farmers market, sharing a communal wood-fired oven with three other home bakers.

  • 2021

    Waitlists and a bigger mixer

    Word spreads. Loaves sell out within the hour and the market stall grows a standing waitlist for the following week.

  • 2022

    Miller Street opens

    Hearth & Rye moves into its own space, with a custom-built wood-fired hearth oven and a small coffee counter.

  • 2026

    Same ovens, same early mornings

    The bakery still opens before sunrise and still mills a portion of its own grain — the methods haven't changed, just the number of loaves.

What we care about

Slow fermentation

Most of our doughs rest for 24 to 72 hours before baking — it's slower, but it's what gives the bread its flavor and crust.

Local grain

A portion of our flour is milled in-house from grain grown within a day's drive of the bakery.

Small batches

We'd rather sell out by early afternoon than bake more than the oven can do justice to in one round.
